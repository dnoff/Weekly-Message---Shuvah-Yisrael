IVision of Dry Bones

Ezekiel 37:1–3
1 The hand of ADONAI was upon me. The Ruach ADONAI carried me out and set me down in the middle of the valley. It was full of bones. 2 He led me all around them. Behold, there were very many on the floor of the valley. Behold, they were very dry. 3 Then He said to me, “Son of man, can these bones live?” I answered, “ADONAI Elohim, You know.”

What can we understand from this?

  • First this is a vision, of things to come.
  • There are many bones, and they are very dry.
  • The bones are spread out, all in the valley.
  • To be very dry is to be long dead — no flesh or tendons, only full death.

AGod Himself poses the question

Ezekiel 37:3–6
3 Then He said to me, “Son of man, can these bones live?” I answered, “ADONAI Elohim, You know.” 4 “Prophesy over these bones,” He said to me. “Say to them: ‘Dry bones, hear the word of ADONAI!’ 5 Thus says ADONAI Elohim to these bones: ‘Behold, I will cause Ruach to enter you, so you will live. 6 I will attach tendons to you, bring flesh on you and cover you with skin. Then I will put breath in you. You will live. You will know that I am ADONAI.’”

  • The Lord directly asks: Can these bones come to life?
  • It seems impossible. “Lord, You know” is a simple way for Ezekiel to leave it in His hands.
  • A promise is made by the Lord God, to reform the bones, to make them new.

BThe bones come together — flesh, yet no life

Ezekiel 37:7–8
7 So I prophesied just as I was commanded. As I prophesied, there was a noise, and behold, an earthquake. Then the bones came together, bone to its bone. 8 I saw, and behold, there were tendons on them, flesh came up and skin covered them above, but there was no breath in them.

CThe life is in the breath

Life is not given to the bones until the breath of God goes forth and life is restored to the body. Though with flesh and tendons after being apart, still no life until breath.

Ezekiel 37:9–10
9 Then He said to me, “Prophesy to the Ruach. Prophesy, son of man, and say to the Ruach, thus says ADONAI Elohim: ‘Come from the four winds, Ruach! Breathe upon these slain, that they may live.’” 10 So I prophesied just as He commanded me. The Ruach came into them and they lived. They stood up on their feet, a vast army.

IIMeaning of the Vision

The meaning of the vision is given from Ezekiel 37:11–23.

  • A promise that the nation will return after exile.
  • Which exile? Some believe it is the exile from Babylon.
  • It cannot be. Why? Because their sins are forgiven and one king rules over them.
  • This did not happen on their return. No King of David has been seen — except Yeshua.

AYeshua returns to save and reign

Ezekiel 37:24
24 “My servant David will be king over them. They will all have One Shepherd. They will walk in My ordinances and observe My rulings and do them.”

Yeshua returns to reign, a king set over Israel forever.

Zechariah 14:9
9 ADONAI will then be King over all the earth. In that day ADONAI will be Echad and His Name Echad.

Revelation 22:3–5
3 No longer will there be any curse. The throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in the city, and His servants shall serve Him. 4 They shall see His face, and His name shall be on their foreheads. 5 Night shall be no more, and people will have no need for lamplight or sunlight — for ADONAI Elohim will shine on them. And they shall reign forever and ever!

IIIAccusations toward Israel

With all these verses many question: why is the nation of Israel in unbelief? If they returned, why stay this way? Because this is part of God’s plan for His people.

Zechariah 12:10
10 “Then I will pour out on the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and supplication, when they will look toward Me whom they pierced. They will mourn for him as one mourns for an only son and grieve bitterly for him, as one grieves for a firstborn.”

Romans 11:26–27
26 and in this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written, “The Deliverer shall come out of Zion. He shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob. 27 And this is My covenant with them, when I take away their sins.”

(Paul quotes Psalm 14:7 and Isaiah 27:9.)
